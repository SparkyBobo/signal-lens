# ADR: Signal Lens — Product Identity and Conceptual Architecture

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-11-22

## Context

The project began as software for measuring and characterizing audio amplifiers. Its purpose is to turn measurement data into useful engineering understanding through analysis, visualization, and reporting.

During design discussions, the intended scope expanded beyond amplifier measurements. The underlying approach could also support digital electronics, timing analysis, protocol decoding, and other engineering measurement domains.

The project should have an identity that reflects this broader purpose without requiring the initial implementation to become a general-purpose measurement platform prematurely.

The central idea is that measurements are inputs. The real value is the understanding derived from them.

## Decision

The software will be named **Signal Lens**.

The tagline is:

*Measure. Analyze. Visualize.*

The name reflects the idea that different analytical perspectives, or lenses, can be applied to measurement data to reveal different aspects of system behavior.

The software will be organized conceptually around three stages:

### 1. Measure

Acquire and import measurement data.

Responsibilities include:
- Reading measurement files and instrument output.
- Supporting different measurement sources.
- Importing and normalizing source-specific data.
- Preserving the information needed for subsequent analysis.

### 2. Profile

Analyze measurements and develop a characterization of the measured system.

Responsibilities include:
- Domain-specific calculations.
- Analysis and interpretation.
- Applying measurement-specific rules and assumptions.
- Producing derived data suitable for visualization and reporting.

A profile describes the behavior and characteristics revealed by the measurements.

### 3. Report

Communicate the results in a useful and understandable form.

Responsibilities include:
- Generating plots and visualizations.
- Presenting calculated results.
- Comparing measurements.
- Combining results into coherent reports.
- Supporting readable, reproducible engineering documentation.

Visualization is an integral part of reporting, rather than a separate top-level processing stage.

## Architectural Direction

Signal Lens will support specialized measurement pipelines.

Each measurement type will have its own appropriate:
- Importer
- Profiling and analysis logic
- Reporting and visualization logic

Multiple measurement pipelines can be composed into a larger report.

This allows specialized analysis to remain independent while supporting combined presentations of a system's behavior.

The architecture should permit new measurement domains and analytical lenses to be added without requiring unrelated measurement implementations to be rewritten.

Potential future domains include:

- **Audio:** Frequency response, distortion, noise, power and efficiency.
- **Digital timing:** Designed and captured timing diagrams, setup and hold analysis, clock behavior.
- **Protocol analysis:** UART, SPI, I²C, MIDI, DMX and other communication protocols.
- **Comparative analysis:** Comparing captures, revisions, and measured performance.

These are examples of the intended direction, not commitments for the initial release.

## Scope and Implementation Constraints

The initial implementation remains focused on the amplifier measurement use case.

The architecture should provide reasonable opportunities for extension, but extensibility must not become an excuse to over-engineer the initial solution.

In particular:
- Implement only abstractions justified by the initial use case.
- Avoid forcing fundamentally different measurement types into an unnecessarily universal data model.
- Preserve domain-specific analysis where appropriate.
- Defer generalized functionality until an actual second use case demonstrates its requirements.

The goal is a very good solution to the initial problem that has a credible path to becoming something more.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The product identity is not restricted to audio or amplifier testing.
- The three stages provide a clear conceptual organization.
- Specialized measurement pipelines can evolve independently.
- New analytical lenses can provide different perspectives on shared measurement data.
- Reports can combine multiple sources of engineering evidence.
- The architecture encourages reusable analysis without requiring premature generalization.

### Trade-offs

- Different measurement domains will have different data structures and analysis requirements.
- A common interface between pipelines will need to emerge from actual use cases.
- Reporting multiple measurement types may introduce coordination and consistency requirements.
- The distinction between importing, profiling, and presenting data must remain clear as the implementation grows.

## Naming Rationale

**Signal Lens** was selected over alternatives including Bench Mark, Signal Book, Signal View, and Signal Lenses.

The singular form was chosen deliberately.

The software is one lens through which engineers can better understand measured systems. Individual analytical perspectives can still be referred to as lenses.

The name communicates the product's purpose without prescribing a particular instrument, measurement technique, or engineering domain.

## Summary

Signal Lens is an engineering measurement analysis and reporting application.

Its purpose is to turn measured data into understanding.

**Measure. Analyze. Visualize.**
